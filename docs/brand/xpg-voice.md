# XPG Voice, Language & Tone — distilled from the Church Health Assessment Guide

Source of truth: `XPG Church Health Assessment Guide` (internal brand + consulting guide, 47 sections,
38pp). This file is the working distillation for anyone editing report copy. When the two disagree,
the Guide wins.

Every string a church leader reads — `methodology/report.yaml`, `methodology/copy.yaml`, the
`SYSTEM_PROMPT` in `lib/ai/prose.ts`, section titles, CTAs — is governed by this file.

---

## 1. The posture (Guide §5)

**Beside the leader, not above the leader.** XPG is not a consultant standing outside the church
criticizing it. XPG is an experienced ministry practitioner walking alongside another ministry leader.

The report should never feel like: *"Here is everything wrong with your church."*

It should feel like: *"Here is what God appears to be building, here is what needs strengthening to
steward it well, and here are the most important next steps."*

## 2. The eight voice attributes (Guide §22)

| Attribute | What it means in copy |
|---|---|
| **Pastoral** | Real people and ministry callings sit behind every number. |
| **Strategic** | Move past observation into implication and decision. |
| **Direct** | Name uncomfortable realities. Do not soften findings into meaninglessness. |
| **Hopeful** | Frame challenges as opportunities for stewardship and development. |
| **Kingdom-centered** | The purpose is Gospel effectiveness and disciple-making, not a better org chart. |
| **Practical** | Every recommendation must be implementable. |
| **Relational** | Speak *with* leaders, not *at* leaders. |
| **Executive** | Understand structure, accountability, systems, finances, complexity. |

Held together: **spiritual discernment + organizational wisdom** (§25). Never collapse into either
"they just need to pray more" or "they just need better systems."

## 3. Challenge without condemnation (Guide §23)

Truth must never become shame. The Guide's own before/after pairs:

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| "Your leadership is dysfunctional." | "The data suggests greater leadership alignment is needed before the organization takes its next major step." |
| "Your church has terrible follow-up." | "The church appears effective at reaching people, but the current follow-up process may not consistently move those relationships beyond Sunday." |
| "Your staff structure doesn't work." | "The current structure may have served the church's previous season well, but greater role and decision clarity will likely be needed for the next stage of growth." |
| "Your volunteers aren't committed." | "Volunteer participation may be less of a commitment problem and more of a pathway, recruitment, ownership, or development opportunity." |

Pattern: **hedge the interpretation, not the finding.** "appears / may / suggests / likely" attach to
what the data *means*; the number itself stays stated plainly.

## 4. Language to use frequently (Guide §24)

Kingdom · discipleship · stewardship · health · clarity · alignment · capacity · culture · leadership
development · leadership multiplication · next step · ownership · opportunity · strategic priority ·
growth ceiling · capacity gap · growth constraint · healthy foundation · organizational maturity ·
ministry effectiveness · sustainable growth · multiplication · equipping · developing · empowering ·
reproducing · strengthening · protecting · building · scaling · stewarding

## 5. Language to limit (Guide §25)

Corporate consulting register: human capital optimization, enterprise transformation, organizational
restructuring initiative, consumer acquisition, customer journey, market penetration, performance
management ecosystem.

Churches are organizations, but they are not *merely* organizations. Use executive expertise without
stripping ministry of its spiritual nature.

**Also avoid** (derived from §23 + §44, and the failure mode this codebase currently exhibits):
mechanical//diagnostic-machine register applied to people — "broken", "failed", "absent", "the chain",
"raises numbers once and changes nothing", "do not work on these". These read as verdicts on a
leader, not as stewardship framing. Prefer growth constraint, capacity gap, growth ceiling, not yet
consistent, ready to carry, thin under load.

## 6. Discipleship language (Guide §21)

Move away from → toward:

- Engagement → **transformation**
- Getting people connected to programs → **helping people take their next step toward Jesus**
- Collecting decisions → **developing disciples**

Key question: *"How can we help this person take their next step toward Jesus?"*

## 7. Score interpretation bands (Guide §18) — normative

| Range | Band language |
|---|---|
| 85–100% | **Strength to protect + multiply.** A healthy organizational asset. Ask how to protect, reproduce and leverage it. |
| 75–84% | **Healthy + maturing.** Generally healthy; opportunities to strengthen consistency or prepare for the next stage of growth. |
| 60–74% | **Potential growth constraint.** Deserves strategic attention. Not a crisis, but it will increasingly limit the church as complexity or attendance increases. |
| Below 60% | **Strategic priority.** Requires intentional leadership attention. Determine root causes and establish clear next actions. |

Scores must **never become character judgments**. Anything below 75% deserves investigation, but not
everything below 75% is a top priority. Cap at ~**three** major strategic priorities. Churches do not
need 27 recommendations; they need clarity on what matters most next.

## 8. The report writing formula (Guide §26)

Every major finding: **Observation → Interpretation → Why It Matters → Strategic Direction → First
Action.**

- *Observation* — what does the data indicate?
- *Interpretation* — what may be happening beneath the number?
- *Why it matters* — how could this affect discipleship, leadership, culture, or growth?
- *Strategic direction* — what should leadership consider doing?
- *First action* — what should happen next?

## 9. Every report identifies three things (Guide §27)

1. **Protect** — what is clearly healthy. Do not overlook strengths because the consultant is looking
   for problems. Celebrate what God is already doing.
2. **Strengthen** — what works but needs greater consistency or capacity.
3. **Build** — what must be created or significantly redesigned for the next season.

This creates a balanced assessment. Strengths are named **first** (§44 ALWAYS).

## 10. Hard AI rules for generated report copy (Guide §44)

**Never:** diagnose solely from the composite score · assume bigger equals healthier · compare every
church to a megachurch · recommend programs because they are popular · shame pastors or staff · label
individuals as dysfunctional · make denominational assumptions · treat perception data as
unquestionable fact · present speculation as fact · recommend hiring before examining ownership and
structure · give every low-scoring area equal priority · overwhelm leadership with dozens of
recommendations · separate organizational strategy from discipleship and Kingdom mission.

**Always:** identify strengths first · consider church context · examine leadership alignment ·
identify significant variance · distinguish data from interpretation · explain why findings matter ·
prioritize · connect recommendations to discipleship and Kingdom impact · give actionable next steps ·
maintain pastoral sensitivity · speak with executive clarity · look for multiplication.

## 11. Interpreting variance, not just level (Guide §16, §19)

The value of a 360 is not the average. The key signal is **where different levels of leadership
experience the church differently**.

- Low score + high agreement → the organization already recognizes it; leadership can move quickly.
- Low score + high disagreement → uneven ministry experience, communication gaps, silos, inconsistent
  systems, different expectations. Investigate before prescribing.
- High score + high agreement → a genuine strength. Protect and reproduce it.
- High score + high disagreement → the average may be misleading. Investigate the variance.
- Exec high / frontline low → *"Leadership appears aligned around the intention, while frontline
  responses suggest the experience has not yet become consistently embedded throughout the
  organization."*
- Frontline high / exec low → leaders may be overly critical, or strong informal ministry may exist
  despite incomplete systems. Do not assume either group is correct.

## 12. Growth exposes; it does not always create (Guide §9)

Frame constraints as **"a capacity issue created by the church entering a new season,"** not
*"leadership has failed."* What worked at 150 breaks at 700 — that is maturation, not failure.

Related (§8): *Protect the Heart. Scale the Culture. Multiply the Mission.* Do not accidentally
destroy what caused the church to grow while solving the problems growth created.

## 13. The mantra (Guide §46) and the ultimate question (Guide §47)

> Protect what is healthy. Clarify what is confusing. Align what is disconnected. Strengthen what is
> limiting growth. Build what the next season requires. Multiply what advances the Kingdom.

Every assessment should eventually answer: **"What does this church need to strengthen now so it can
faithfully steward where God appears to be taking it next?"**

## 14. What the brand should feel like (Guide §42)

When a Pastor or Executive Pastor reads an XPG report, the desired reaction is: *"They understand
ministry. They understand leadership. They understand what growing churches actually experience. They
didn't just give us generic church advice. They saw things we couldn't see. They challenged us without
tearing us down. We know what to do next."*

# Audit — where the current report copy misses this

Assessed against `methodology/copy.yaml` and `methodology/report.yaml` at commit `d6df45b`.

## A. Tone gaps (voice)

1. **Machine register applied to a church.** "chain", "broken", "absent", "below the point where a
   stage is working", "running on what little gets through". The Guide's vocabulary for the same
   finding is *growth constraint / capacity gap / growth ceiling / not yet consistent*. `copy.yaml`
   `dossier.reading.stage.severe` — "This is not underperforming, it is absent" — is the sharpest
   offender.
2. **Commanding, not walking alongside.** `blocks.do_not_work_on`: "Do not work on these yet, they are
   symptoms of the constraint above." Speaks *at* the leader. §5 requires *with*.
3. **Dismissive of the leader's prior spend.** `blocks.cost` / `report.yaml` s9: "raises numbers once
   and changes nothing."
4. **Zero Kingdom / discipleship / stewardship vocabulary anywhere in the copy layer.** §44 forbids
   separating organizational strategy from discipleship and Kingdom mission; the current copy is a
   pure org-diagnostic voice.
5. **No Protect / Strengthen / Build spine** (§27), and strengths (s5) are placed *after* the
   constraint verdict (s4) rather than first (§44 ALWAYS).
6. **Findings stated as fact, not interpretation** (§44 "distinguish data from interpretation",
   "treat perception data as unquestionable fact"). Current copy asserts: "Your primary constraint
   *is* X." Guide register: "The data suggests…", "appears to…", "may…".
7. **Band names do not match §18.** `rules.yaml` tiers are `healthy_ready / healthy_stretched /
   strained / at_risk`; the Guide's normative bands are *Strength to Protect + Multiply / Healthy +
   Maturing / Potential Growth Constraint / Strategic Priority*. Worth reconciling deliberately —
   these are different axes (whole-church tier vs per-area score band), so decide whether to align
   names or keep both and document why.
8. **`style_spine` says "No em-dashes" but the copy is full of them** (`copy.yaml` dossier.reading,
   xpg_read, beats). Internal contradiction: the AI is instructed to strip a punctuation mark the
   deterministic copy uses everywhere.

## C. The "Overall" label ask

`report.yaml` s2/s3/s12 already say "Overall health sits at…" / "Overall {overall_percent} out of
100". The number that is **missing** the word is the hero score rendered at the top of the report —
`lib/report/charts.ts` `verdict_block.hero` (`score` + `tierName`, no label field), drawn by
`lib/report/pdf/charts.tsx`, `app/app/[churchId]/diagnosis/report/charts.tsx` and
`report-cover.tsx`. That hero needs an "Overall" label next to / above the number in **both** the web
report and the PDF, kept byte-consistent between the two surfaces.

# Gotchas for whoever implements this

- `methodology/report.yaml` `version` feeds the report cache key (`lib/report/report-hash.ts`).
  Changing copy without bumping it serves stale reports. `copy.yaml` has its own `version`.
- `report.yaml` `banned_phrases` are keyed to the *current* archetype thesis framings. Rewriting the
  thesis sentences without updating `banned_phrases` silently breaks gate family 3.
- `required_mentions` (`tier_name`, `primary_name`, `overall_percent`) are enforced by
  `lib/ai/section-gates.ts`. Any rewrite must keep those tokens present in the rewritten template.
- `lib/ai/section-gates.ts:40` hardcodes the "…out of 100" scale phrasing. Changing that wording in
  the templates needs a matching change there.
- `passesFactCheck` in `lib/ai/prose.ts` pins numbers per field. Copy rewrites that move a number
  between blocks will route every report to deterministic prose.
- Tests assert on exact strings: `tests/methodology/report-yaml.test.ts` plus the report snapshot
  tests under `tests/report/`. Expect churn; run the full suite.
- Standing repo guardrails: never run `npm run test:db` or `supabase db push|reset`; never push to
  `master` or merge without Natalie; stage explicit paths only, never `.claude/`.
